### Description ###
A can is evacuated with a vacuum pump. Eventually the atmospheric pressure is too big that it crushes the can. Demonstrates the crushing force that the air around us constantly exerts on all surfaces.

### Equipment ###
* Empty can
* Vacuum pump
* Tubing for optimal seal with the chosen can.
  
### Instruction ###
Place the tubing in the container opening. Make a good seal and turn on the pump. Whatch as the air is evacuated the can gets crushed by the atmosphere pressure.

### Physics ###
Under normal atmospheric conditions, the air inside the container push outward with the exact same force that the outside ambient air pushes inward. This equilibrium keeps the container structurally stable. When the vacuum pump turns on, it evacuates the internal air, lowering the density and pressure inside the container. This creates a massive net pressure difference between the interior and exterior environments. The exterior atmospheric pressure effectively pushes down on every square inch of the container's surface area. The cumulative structural load instantly exceeds the strength of the thin metal or plastic walls, forcing the container to implode.

### Concepts ###
* Atmospheric Pressure: The force per unit area exerted against a surface by the weight of the air above that surface in the Earth's atmosphere.
* Vacuum: A space completely or partially devoid of matter, resulting in an localized environment with less pressure than the surrounding ambient space.
