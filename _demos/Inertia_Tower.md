An experiment involving a stack of discs and a ruler is a classic demonstration of Newton's First Law of Motion, also known as the Law of Inertia.

### Instruction ###
1) Stack the Discs: Place 5 to 7 flat, identical discs in a neat, vertical column on a smooth table.
2) Position the Ruler: Hold a flat plastic or wooden ruler horizontally by one end, keeping it level with the table.
3) The Action: Flick or swing the ruler quickly and sharply across the table, aiming directly at the bottom-most disc in the stack.

### The physics ###
Newton's First Law states that an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion, unless acted upon by an external force.
1) The Bottom Disc: The ruler applies a direct, sudden horizontal force to the bottom disc. Because the force overcomes the disc's inertia, it flies out of the stack.
2) The Upper Discs: Because you move the ruler so fast, the friction between the bottom disc and the one above it doesn't have enough time to transfer significant horizontal force to the rest of the stack.
3) Inertia & Gravity: Due to inertia, the upper discs "want" to stay right where they are. Once the bottom disc is gone, the external force of gravity pulls the rest of the stack straight down into the empty space.
