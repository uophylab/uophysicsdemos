### Description ###
Rapid cooling of steam will cause a can to be crushed by the surrounding air pressure.

### Equipment ###
* 4L beaker
* water/ice
* Empty soda can
* tongs
* heat source(torch or hot plate)

### Instruction ###
Fill the beaker with cold water(optional: add ice). Fill the bottom of the can with approx. 1 cm of water. Holding the can with tongs, heat the can from below. Once the water in can is boiling and the can is filled with steam, dip the can the beaker with opening facing down, sealing the steam inside the can with the water. The can should be crushed almost instantly.

### Physics ###
When the shallow pool of water boils, it transitions from a liquid into a gas (steam). As the water vapour expands, it physically drives all the original, heavy air molecules out through the open tab, filling the inside of the can exclusively with hot steam at standard atmospheric pressure. Flipping the can upside down into ice water creates a dual effect. It seals the opening against the outside world and exposes the hot steam to a sudden drop in temperature. This thermal shock causes the gaseous water vapour to instantly condense back into a tiny droplet of liquid water. Because liquid water occupies a tiny fraction of the volume of gaseous steam, the sudden condensation leaves the inside of the sealed can almost entirely empty. This creates a powerful, sudden vacuum, dropping the internal pressure to near zero. With the internal pressure gone, there is no outward force to balance the weight of the air outside. The surrounding atmospheric pressure presses heavily on the outer walls, instantly buckling the weak aluminum shell.


### Concepts ###
* Phase Change: The physical transformation of matter from one state to another.
* Vapour Pressure: The pressure exerted by a vapour in thermodynamic equilibrium.
* Ideal Gas Law Interaction (\(PV = nRT\)): A demonstration of how an immediate drop in temperature and a massive drop in gaseous particle count turning to liquid) forces a catastrophic drop in internal pressure.
