### Description ###
A solid brass ball passes easily through a matching brass ring at room temperature, but gets stuck after being heated with a torch due to thermal expansion.

### Equipment ###
* Ball and ring apparatus (Gravesande's ring)
* Propane torch or Bunsen burner
* Beaker of cold water (optional, for rapid cooling)

### Instruction ###
* Room temperature: Show that the inner diameter of the metal ring is machined to be just slightly larger than the diameter of the metal ball, allowing the ball to pass smoothly through the ring.
* Heating the ball: Hold the metal ball in the flame for 1 to 2 minutes to increase its temperature and volume. Attempt to pass it through the unheated ring; the expanded ball will now be too wide and get stuck on top.
* Cooling the ball: Let the ball cool back to room temperature (or submerge it in cold water) so the metal contracts back to its original size, allowing it to fit through the ring once again.
* Heating the ring (optional): Heat both the ball and the ring simultaneously to demonstrate that the inner hole of the ring expands outward, allowing the heated ball to pass through.

### Physics ###
Adding heat increases the kinetic energy of the atoms inside the solid metal lattice, causing them to vibrate faster and with greater amplitude. Because interatomic bonds are asymmetric—resisting compression more strongly than stretching—the average distance between neighboring atoms increases. This microscopic spacing increase causes the entire object to expand uniformly in all three dimensions, enlarging the diameter of the sphere (and expanding the inner opening of the ring outward if heated).

### Concepts ###
* Thermal expansion: the tendency of matter to change its shape, area, and volume in response to a change in temperature due to increased atomic vibration and spacing.
* Volumetric expansion: the three-dimensional increase in overall volume of a solid ($\Delta V = \beta V_0 \Delta T$) as it is heated.
