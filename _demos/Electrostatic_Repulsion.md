### Description ###
Two standard ping pong balls suspended side-by-side from a Van de Graaff generator repel each other and swing apart at a wide angle when the generator is switched on.

### Equipment ###
* Van de Graaff generator
* Two ping pong ball setup
* Grounding wand

### Instruction ###
* Connect the two ping pong balls setup to the Van de Graaff.
* Turn on the Van de Graaff generator. As charge accumulates on each ping pong ball, observe them push apart and suspend in a V-shape.
* Turn off the generator and touch the grounding wand to the dome. Watch the two balls drop back together as the charge dissipates.

### Physics ###
When the Van de Graaff generator runs, its dome builds up a high electric potential, ionizing the surrounding air and depositing static charge onto the outer plastic surfaces of the hanging ping pong balls. Because both balls acquire the same polarity of static charge, they exert a mutual outward electrostatic repulsive force on one another according to Coulomb's Law. The lightweight balls swing apart until this horizontal repulsion balances the downward pull of gravity. Grounding the dome removes the electric field, allowing the balls to return to rest.

### Concepts ###
* Electrostatic repulsion: objects carrying the same sign of electric charge repel each other.
* Static charge transfer: high voltage on the dome deposits excess electrons onto nearby lightweight surfaces.
* Coulomb's Law: the repulsive electrostatic force between two charged objects is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
