### Description ###
A Peltier thermoelectric module sandwiched between two metal heat sinks—one placed in hot water and the other in ice water—generates an electric current from the temperature difference to spin a small electric motor.

### Equipment ###
* Thermoelectric converter / Peltier demonstration unit (Peltier module mounted between two aluminum heat sink legs with an attached low-voltage DC motor and propeller)
* Two insulated cups or glass beakers
* Hot water (near boiling)
* Cold water with ice cubes

### Instruction ###
* Initial setup: Fill one beaker with hot water and the second beaker with ice water, placing them side by side on a flat surface.
* Creating the temperature gradient: Submerge one aluminum leg of the thermoelectric unit into the hot water beaker and the other leg into the ice water beaker simultaneously.
* Running the motor: Wait 10 to 20 seconds for heat to conduct through the metal legs across the Peltier junction. Once a sufficient temperature difference is established, the propeller will begin spinning continuously.
* Reversing polarity (optional): Remove the unit, wipe the legs dry, and swap their positions so the hot and cold sides are reversed. Observe the propeller spinning in the opposite direction as the current reverses.

### Physics ###
The demonstration operates as a solid-state heat engine utilizing the Seebeck effect. Inside the Peltier module, alternating p-type and n-type semiconductor pellets are connected electrically in series and thermally in parallel. When one side is heated and the other is cooled, charge carriers (electrons in n-type and holes in p-type semiconductors) diffuse faster from the hot junction toward the cold junction. This thermal diffusion creates a net buildup of charge at the cold side, establishing an electric potential difference (voltage). When connected to the small DC motor, this voltage drives a continuous electric current that converts thermal energy directly into mechanical rotation without any moving fluid or pistons.

### Concepts ###
* Seebeck effect: the direct conversion of a temperature difference between two dissimilar conductors or semiconductors into an electric voltage.
* Solid-state heat engine: a device that generates mechanical work from heat flow between a high-temperature source and a low-temperature sink without using a traditional working gas or liquid.
* Second Law of Thermodynamics: electrical work is only generated while heat actively flows from the hot reservoir to the cold reservoir; once the water temperatures equalize, the motor stops.
