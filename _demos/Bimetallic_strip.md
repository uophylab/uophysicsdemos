### Description ###
A straight metal strip made of two different metals bonded together bends into a sharp curve when heated over a flame and straightens back out as it cools.

### Equipment ###
* Bimetallic strip (typically brass and steel/invar bonded together, mounted on an insulated handle)
* Heat source (Bunsen burner, alcohol lamp, or propane torch)
* Beaker of cold water or ice bath (optional, for rapid cooling or reverse bending)
* Heat-resistant mat

### Instruction ###
* Room temperature: Hold up the bimetallic strip at room temperature to show that both metal layers are flat and straight.
* Heating the strip: Hold the metal blade in a flame for 10 to 20 seconds, moving it gently to heat it evenly. Observe the strip curving noticeably toward one side as its temperature rises.
* Cooling the strip: Remove the strip from the flame and let it cool in the air (or dip it into room-temperature water). Watch it gradually straighten back to its original flat shape.
* Extreme cooling (optional): Place the strip in ice water or liquid nitrogen to show that it bends in the opposite direction when cooled below room temperature.

### Physics ###
A bimetallic strip consists of two different metals—commonly brass and steel—welded or riveted face-to-face along their entire length. Different metals have different coefficients of linear thermal expansion ($\alpha$), meaning they expand by different amounts for the same temperature increase. Brass expands nearly twice as much as steel when heated. Because the two layers are rigidly bonded together and cannot slide past one another, the faster-expanding brass layer is forced to take the outside of a curve, while the slower-expanding steel layer stays on the inside. When cooled below room temperature, brass contracts more than steel, causing the strip to bend in the opposite direction.

### Concepts ###
* Differential thermal expansion: different materials expand and contract at distinct rates when subjected to the same change in temperature ($\Delta L = \alpha L_0 \Delta T$).
* Coefficient of linear expansion ($\alpha$): a material-specific constant measuring the fractional change in length per degree of temperature change.
* Thermal switch / thermostat mechanism: the bending motion of a bimetallic strip can be used to physically open or close an electrical contact at a specific temperature.
