### Description ###
A gyroscope rotates about its axis. A top stays upright.


### Equipment ###
* various small gyroscopes and spinning tops

### Instruction ###
Spin the tops or gyroscopes and lay them on the table. They will rotate and demonstrate properties of rotational dynamics.


### The physics ###
When a top or gyroscope spins rapidly, it gains angular momentum. This momentum is a vector quantity that points directly along the axis of rotation. According to Newton's laws of rotational motion, an object's spin axis will rigidly maintain its orientation in space unless acted upon by an external torque. For a stationary, non-spinning top, gravity immediately pulls its centre of mass downward, causing it to fall over. For a spinning top, however, its massive angular momentum strongly resists this gravitational tipping force.

### Concepts ###
* Angular Momentum: A vector quantity tracking the intensity and directional axis of a spinning object. It points perpendicular to the rotation plane along the axle.
* Torque: The rotational twisting force. In this demo, it is created by the gravitational force acting at a distance from the pivot point where the rope is tied.
* Gyroscopic Precession: The continuous, perpendicular change in the orientation of a spinning object's rotation axis when a continuous external torque is applied.
* Rotational Inertia: The structural property of the bicycle wheel that resists changes to its rotation speed or axis direction, maximized by pushing the wheel's mass out to the tire rim.
