### Description ###
Many vertical tubes filled with liquid are connected to a horizontal tube with varying diameter. The vertical tubes have their other end connected together. When air is blown in the horizontal tube the difference in pressure in change the level of the liquid in the tubes accordong to Bernoulli's law

### Equipment ###
* Venturi tubes
* Coloured alcohol
* Support with clamps
* Air pump
* Ruled paper (optional)

### Instruction ###
1. Install the Venturi tube on the support with the clamps.
2. (Optional) Attach ruled paper behind the vertical tube to make the change in height more visible.
3. Fill with the alcohol so that the vertical tubes are half full.
4. Attach the air blower at one end of the horizontal tube.
5. Turn the air blower on.

### Physics ###
The total mass flowing through the pipe per second must remain constant because air is an non-compressible fluid. When the air passes from a wide section into a constricted, narrow section, the same volume of air is forced to squeeze through a smaller area, causing its linear velocity to drastically increase. According to Bernoulli's equation, as the velocity of a fluid increases, its static pressure must simultaneously decrease to conserve total mechanical energy.
Because the air is moving at its highest speed through the narrow constriction, a localized low-pressure zone (a partial vacuum) is formed right at that throat. The higher, static atmospheric pressure pushing down on the interconnected fluid reservoirs forces the coloured alcohol up into the low-pressure vertical column to balance out the suction force.

### Concepts ###
* Bernoulli's Principle: The fundamental fluid dynamics law stating that an increase in the speed of a fluid occurs simultaneously with a decrease in static pressure or fluid potential energy.
* The Venturi Effect: The specific physical phenomenon where a fluid's pressure drops significantly as it flows through a constricted section of a pipe. Where liquid moves away from high-pressure zones toward low-pressure vacuums.
* Equation of Continuity: The principle of conservation of mass in fluids, dictating that a reduction in cross-sectional area forces a proportional increase in fluid velocity.
