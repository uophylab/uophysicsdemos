### Description ###
A weighted thin metal wire slowly passes completely through a solid block of ice without cutting it into two separate pieces.

### Equipment ###
* Large block of ice
* Thin metal wire (such as copper wire)
* Two heavy weights (e.g., 1 kg to 2 kg masses)
* Elevated support stand or two stools (to allow the weights to hang freely)
* Drip tray or basin (to catch meltwater)

### Instruction ###
* Initial setup: Place the block of ice across a support gap over a drip tray so the center of the block is suspended in the air.
* Applying pressure: Tie a heavy weight to each end of the thin metal wire and drape the wire over the top center of the ice block so the weights hang down on either side.
* Observing regelation: Over the course of 30 to 60 minutes, watch as the wire steadily sinks down through the ice block. Once the wire passes completely through and drops below, inspect or lift the ice block to show it remains a single, solid piece.

### Physics ###
Unlike most substances, water expands when it freezes, meaning liquid water is denser than solid ice. Because of this unique property, applying intense localized pressure lowers the melting point of ice. The thin wire concentrates the downward force of the heavy weights onto a tiny surface area, creating high pressure that melts the ice directly beneath it. As the wire sinks into the meltwater, the water flows above the wire where the pressure returns to normal. Once relieved of pressure, the water's freezing point rises back to $0^\circ\text{C}$, and the surrounding cold ice absorbs its latent heat—conducting heat through the metal wire—causing the water to refreeze into solid ice behind the wire.

### Concepts ###
* Regelation: the phenomenon of ice melting under intense pressure and refreezing once the pressure is reduced.
* Pressure-dependent melting point: for substances that expand upon freezing (like water), increasing external pressure lowers the temperature at which the solid transitions into a liquid.
* Latent heat transfer: thermal energy released by the refreezing water above the wire conducts through the metal wire to help melt the ice below it.
