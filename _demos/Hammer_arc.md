### Description ###
A hammer is balanced on an arc of metal which is held by the demonstrator's finger. Also known as the skyhook.


### Equipment ###
* Hammer
* Metal arc

### Instruction ###
Place the arc into the hammer's forked side. Balance the flat end of the arc on your finger. (see images and movies)

### Physics ###
Looking at the setup, it intuitively feels like the heavy handle sticking out should cause the structure to flip backward. However, because the metal arc is aggressively curved, it physically forces the heavy head and handle of the hammer to swing forward, looping far back beneath the pivot point. For any system to achieve static balance on a small fulcrum, its net centre of mass must lie directly along a vertical line slicing straight through the point of support. In this specific configuration, the heavy head of the hammer acts as a massive counterweight, pulling the true centre of mass of the combined "hammer-arc system" forward until it resides in empty space directly under your finger. Because the centre of mass hangs below the support point on your finger, the setup mimics a pendulum. If you tilt or jog the hammer, its centre of mass is forced to swing upward. Gravity instantly exerts a naturally correcting restoring torque that pulls the centre of mass back down to its lowest possible point, stabilizing the structure against wobbles.

### Concepts ###
* Center of Mass: The exact point where the entire mass of a multi-part system averages out. An object can easily balance on a single point as long as its net centre of mass is vertically aligned with that point.
* Restoring Torque: The rotational force generated by gravity when the balanced system is disturbed, acting to swing the centre of mass straight back beneath the support finger.
