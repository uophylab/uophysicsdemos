### Description ###
A small piece of cotton placed at the bottom of a sealed transparent cylinder ignites into a bright flash when a tight-fitting plunger is rapidly slammed downward, compressing the air inside.

### Equipment ###
* Fire syringe apparatus (thick-walled acrylic or glass compression cylinder with a heavy-duty piston and rubber O-rings)
* Small tuft of dry cotton fiber or flash cotton (nitrocellulose)
* Silicone grease or petroleum jelly (for lubricating the O-rings)
* Cleaning rod or tweezers (to remove ash after firing)

### Instruction ###
* Initial setup: Inspect the rubber O-rings on the piston and apply a thin coat of silicone grease to ensure an airtight seal. Drop a tiny, loose tuft of dry cotton fiber into the bottom of the clean cylinder.
* Compressing the air: Place the base of the syringe firmly on a sturdy table. Insert the plunger into the top of the cylinder, then slam the handle straight down with a quick, forceful motion.
* Observing ignition: Watch the bottom of the transparent tube at the instant of full compression; a bright flash of light will appear as the cotton ignites in the superheated air.
* Resetting: Pull the plunger back up immediately to clear the smoke, and use a cleaning rod to remove any charred residue before repeating.


### Physics ###
Slamming the plunger downward performs mechanical work on the trapped air inside the cylinder. Because the compression happens in a fraction of a second, virtually no heat has time to escape through the walls of the tube—making it a nearly adiabatic process ($Q \approx 0$). According to the First Law of Thermodynamics ($\Delta U = Q - W$), all of the mechanical work done on the gas goes directly into increasing its internal energy ($U$). For an ideal gas, internal energy is directly proportional to absolute temperature, so rapidly reducing the air's volume to roughly one-fifteenth of its original size causes its temperature to spike above $250^\circ\text{C}$ ($480^\circ\text{F}$)—exceeding the autoignition temperature of the cotton.

### Concepts ###
* Adiabatic compression: a rapid compression of a gas during which no thermal energy enters or leaves the system, resulting in a sharp rise in gas temperature.
* First Law of Thermodynamics: energy is conserved in thermodynamic systems ($\Delta U = Q - W$), meaning mechanical work performed on an insulated gas increases its internal thermal energy.
* Diesel engine principle: diesel engines ignite fuel without spark plugs by using high-ratio adiabatic compression to superheat air inside the cylinder.
