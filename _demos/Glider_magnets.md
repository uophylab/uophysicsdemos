### Description ###
Magnets on the end of two gliders on an air track simulate friction-less elastic and inelastic collisions.

### Equipment ###
* air track with pump and tubing, power cable
two gliders
magnet attachments

### Instruction ###
Connect the air track to the pump and turn it on. Insert the magnetic attachments into the gliders and softly collide them. Magnets with similar polarity will repel (elastic collision) while those with opposite polarity with attract (inelastic collision).

### Physics ###
* Conservation of Linear Momentum: In both scenarios, the total linear momentum of the system before the collision identically equals the total momentum after the collision. Because the air cushion eliminates external frictional forces, the net external force is zero.
* Elastic Collisions (Repelling Magnets): In this setup, both total momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved. The kinetic energy of the moving glider is temporarily stored as magnetic potential energy as the fields compress, then fully released back into kinetic energy as they push apart. No energy is lost to heat, sound, or physical damage.
* Inelastic Collisions (Attracting Magnets): While total momentum remains strictly conserved, kinetic energy is not conserved. When the gliders stick together, a portion of the initial kinetic energy is permanently transformed into internal energy, sound, and thermal energy during the impact. Because they lock together and move at the exact same final velocity, this represents a collision.

### Concepts ###
* Conservation of Momentum: The total momentum of an isolated system remains constant over time.
* Elastic Collision: A collision where there is no net loss in total kinetic energy within the system.
* Inelastic Collision: A collision where the objects stick together upon impact, resulting in the maximum possible loss of system kinetic energy.
* Magnetic Potential Energy: The mechanical energy stored within the magnetic fields, which replaces the physical deformation of standard structural springs in this demo.
