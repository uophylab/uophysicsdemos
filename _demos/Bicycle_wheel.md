### Description ###
1) A large bicycle wheel works as a spinning top, staying upright by conservation of rotational energy.
2) The bicycle wheel is rotating rapidly and is hanging from a rope attached to a rod protruding from the wheel's center. The wheel begins to torsionally rotate.


### Equipment ###
* bicycle wheel
* motor
* c-clamp
* power for the motor
* rope

### Instruction ###
1) Spin the bicycle wheel using the motor and then lay it horizontally on the floor on its handle. It should stay upright and spinning.
2) Spin the bicycle wheel using the motor then let it hang from the rope. The wheel will stay upright and rotate torsionally.

### The physics ###
1) Gyroscopic Stability (Action 1): A spinning wheel possesses a massive amount of angular momentum pointing straight along its axle. According to Newton's laws for rotation, the orientation of this momentum vector cannot change unless acted upon by an external torque. When placed on the floor, its high rotational momentum keeps the spin axis locked in place, preventing the wheel from tipping over.
2) Gyroscopic Precession (Action 2): When the wheel is suspended by only one side of its axle, gravity pulls downward on the unsupported side, creating an external torque. Intuitively, you expect the wheel to fall over. However, because torque equals the change in angular momentum, the gravitational torque doesn't make the wheel tip downward—it pulls the angular momentum vector sideways. The result is precession. The wheel moves perpendicularly to both the downward pull of gravity and its own spin axis, causing the entire wheel to trace out a slow, horizontal circle around the suspension rope while maintaining its vertical stance.

### Concepts ###
* Angular Momentum: A vector quantity tracking the intensity and directional axis of a spinning object. It points perpendicular to the rotation plane along the axle.
• Torque: The rotational twisting force. In this demo, it is created by the gravitational force acting at a distance from the pivot point where the rope is tied.
• Gyroscopic Precession: The continuous, perpendicular change in the orientation of a spinning object's rotation axis when a continuous external torque is applied.
• Rotational Inertia: The structural property of the bicycle wheel that resists changes to its rotation speed or axis direction, maximized by pushing the wheel's mass out to the tire rim.
