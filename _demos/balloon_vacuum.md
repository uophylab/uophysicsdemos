### Description ###
A partially inflated balloon placed inside a vacuum chamber expands dramatically as the surrounding air is pumped out, and shrinks back to its original size when air is let back in.

### Equipment ###
* Vacuum pump
* Bell jar and vacuum base plate
* Rubber balloon (partially inflated and tied)

### Instruction ###
* Initial setup: Inflate a balloon slightly so it is small and loose, tie it off, and place it inside the bell jar on the vacuum plate. Ensure the rubber seal around the rim is clean and airtight.
* Evacuating the chamber: Turn on the vacuum pump to remove the air surrounding the balloon inside the jar. As the external pressure drops, observe the balloon expanding until it fills a large portion of the chamber.
* Releasing the vacuum: Turn off the pump and slowly open the release valve to let atmospheric air rush back into the bell jar. Watch the balloon shrink back down to its original small size.

### Physics ###
A tied balloon maintains a state of equilibrium where the outward pressure of the trapped air inside balances the inward atmospheric pressure outside plus the elastic tension of the rubber. When the vacuum pump removes air from the bell jar, the external pressure surrounding the balloon drops close to zero. Without the outside air pushing inward, the fixed amount of gas trapped inside pushes the rubber walls outward, increasing the balloon's volume until the elastic tension of the stretched rubber balances the reduced internal pressure (following Boyle's Law, $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$).

### Concepts ###
* Boyle's Law: at a constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure ($P \propto 1/V$).
* Atmospheric pressure: the force per unit area exerted by the weight of surrounding air molecules pushing inward on all surfaces.
