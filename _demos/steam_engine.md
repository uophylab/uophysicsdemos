### Description ###
A tabletop model steam engine boils water in a sealed boiler to generate high-pressure steam, driving a reciprocating piston to spin a flywheel.

### Equipment ###
* Model tabletop steam engine
* Distilled water
* Fuel source
* Matches or lighter
* Heat-resistant mat

### Instruction ###
* Initial setup: Fill the boiler roughly two-thirds full with distilled water using a funnel, leaving space at the top for steam to collect. Apply a drop of light oil to the piston rod, crank bearings, and flywheel axle.
* Heating the boiler: Place the lit fuel tray or burner directly beneath the boiler on a heat-resistant mat. Wait 3 to 5 minutes for the water to reach a boil and build up steam pressure (check the sight glass and safety valve).
* Running the engine: Open the steam throttle valve and give the flywheel a gentle flick by hand to clear dead center. The engine will accelerate and run continuously as pressurized steam drives the piston.
* Shutting down: Close the throttle valve, carefully slide out and extinguish the burner, and allow the boiler to cool completely before draining any remaining water.

### Physics ###
A steam engine operates as an external combustion heat engine that relies on the liquid-to-gas phase transition of water. Burning fuel transfers thermal energy into the boiler, providing the latent heat of vaporization needed to turn liquid water into steam. Because steam occupies roughly 1,600 times the volume of liquid water at atmospheric pressure, confining it inside the boiler builds up high pressure. When the throttle opens, this high-pressure steam expands into the cylinder, pushing the piston forward and converting thermal energy into mechanical work via a crank-and-flywheel mechanism. A slide valve then directs the spent, lower-pressure steam out of the exhaust port as the flywheel's momentum returns the piston for the next stroke (approximating the Rankine cycle).

### Concepts ###
* Heat engine: a device that converts thermal energy into mechanical work by exploiting a pressure and temperature difference between a hot boiler and the cooler surrounding atmosphere.
* Latent heat of vaporization: the thermal energy absorbed by liquid water to change phase into steam without raising its temperature, producing massive volumetric expansion.
* Rankine cycle: the fundamental thermodynamic cycle describing how steam engines generate mechanical power through phase changes of a working fluid.
