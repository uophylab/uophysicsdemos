### Description ###
A plunger can be deformed in a way to create a region of low pressure and sticks to surfaces by suction.

### Equipment ###
* Sink Plunger
* Smooth surface (eg. flat copper plate)

### Instruction ###
Push the plunger on the metal surface and observe the suction created on the copper plate.

### Physics ###
When you press the plunger down, you physically force almost all the air molecules out from beneath the rubber dome, flattening its internal volume. When you stop pushing, the elastic rubber naturally tries to spring back into its original dome shape. However, because its edges are sealed tightly against the copper plate, no outside air can rush back in to fill the expanding space. This creates a zone of low partial pressure (a partial vacuum) inside the cup. The pressure inside the cup is now drastically lower than the ambient atmospheric pressure outside the cup.

### Concepts ###
* Suction: The mechanical phenomenon where a fluid pressure differential creates an apparent adhesive holding force between two surfaces.
* Partial Vacuum: An isolated pocket of space containing a significantly lower fluid density and pressure than the surrounding environment.
