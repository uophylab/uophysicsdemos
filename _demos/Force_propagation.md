### Description ###
A hanging ball-bearing is positioned at the end of a fixed metal bar. When the bar the struck on its opposite end by a hammer, the force propagates through the bar from the collision and causes the ball-bearing to swing away.


### Equipment ###
* metal bar
* 2x c-clamps
* hammer
* ball-bearing pendulum
* rods attachment

### Instruction ###
Attach the metal bar to the table using the c-clamps. Place the ball-bearing pendulum on the rods attachment such that the ball is hanging right at the edge and touching the metal bar. Strike the opposite end of the bar with the hammer.

### Physics ###
When the hammer strikes the metal, it compresses the atoms at the point of contact. These compressed atoms push on their neighboring atoms, launching a longitudinal compression wave that travels down the length of the bar. When this high-speed acoustic wave reaches the far boundary of the bar, the energy cannot travel forward through the air with the same efficiency due to an impedance mismatch. However, because the steel ball-bearing is touching the face, the kinetic energy and momentum are efficiently transferred directly into the ball via an elastic collision, similar to the action of a Newton's Cradle.

### Concepts ###
* Longitudinal Stress Wave: A wave in which the displacement of the medium's particles is parallel to the direction of the wave's propagation (identical to how sound travels through a solid).
* Elastic Collision: An encounter between two bodies in which the total kinetic energy and total momentum of the two bodies are fully conserved.
