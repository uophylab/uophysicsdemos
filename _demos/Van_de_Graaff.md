### Description ###
A Van de Graaff generator uses a moving insulating belt to accumulate a high-voltage static electric charge on a hollow metal dome, producing visible sparks when approached by a grounded discharge wand.

### Equipment ###
* Van de Graaff generator
* Grounding wand (smaller metal discharge sphere attached to a ground wire)
* Insulating stool (optional, for safe human-contact demonstrations)

### Instruction ###
* Connect the grounding wand wire firmly to the ground terminal on the base of the generator.
* Turn on the motor to start the internal belt. Allow 10 to 15 seconds for static charge to build up on the outer metal dome.
* Hold the insulated handle of the grounding wand and slowly bring the grounded sphere close to the main dome. Observe bright electric sparks jumping across the air gap with a sharp snapping sound.
* Test various equipment to demonstrate different electrostatic effects.
* Touch the grounding wand directly against the main dome, switch off the motor while keeping the wand in contact, and hold it there for a few seconds until the belt stops completely to ensure no residual charge remains.

### Physics ###
Inside the vertical column, a motor-driven rubber belt rubs against rollers made of dissimilar materials, separating electric charges via the triboelectric effect. Metal comb electrodes at the bottom spray charge onto the moving belt, which carries it upward into the hollow spherical metal dome. Inside the dome, a second metal comb collects the charge. Because electric charges repel one another and distribute exclusively along the outer surface of a conductor (a Faraday cage effect), charge continues to flow onto the outside of the dome regardless of how much charge is already there—building up potentials of tens or hundreds of thousands of volts. When the grounded wand approaches, the intense electric field ionizes the air molecules in the gap, creating a conductive plasma channel for a spark discharge.

### Concepts ###
* Triboelectric effect: the transfer of electric charge between two different materials when they make contact and separate.
* Electrostatic shielding (Faraday cage): excess charge placed inside a hollow conductor migrates entirely to its outer surface, allowing continuous charge buildup.
* Dielectric breakdown: when an electric field becomes strong enough (roughly $3 \times 10^6\text{ V/m}$ in dry air), it strips electrons from air molecules, turning the insulating air into a conductor and producing a spark.
