### Description ###
Suspend two balloon with different gases such that it doesn't rise or fall.

### Equipment ###
* Balloons
* Helium
* String

### Instruction ###
1. Inflate the first balloon with helium and the second balloon to an identical diameter using a hand pump or your breath (ambient air). Tie both tightly.
2. Release the air-filled balloon. Observe that it sinks directly to the floor. Release the helium-filled balloon. Observe that it shoots straight up to the ceiling.
3. Tie a long strand of string to the helium balloon and attached the balloon filled with air.
4. Both balloons are completely suspended in mid-air, demonstrating neutral buoyancy.

### Physics ###
* Both balloons occupy the exact same three-dimensional volume and it displace the same amount of surrounding ambient air. According to Archimedes' principle, this means the atmosphere exerts an identical upward buoyant force on both balloons.
The difference in their behaviour is entirely due to the weight of the gas inside them:
	• The Air Balloon: The air inside the balloon is tightly compressed by the tension of the stretched rubber shell, making it slightly denser than the uncompressed room air. Because its total gravitational weight is greater than the upward buoyant force (F_g > F_b), the net force is downward, and it sinks.
	• The Helium Balloon: Helium gas molecules are significantly lighter and less dense than standard air molecules. The upward buoyant force is initially much stronger than the weight of the balloon shell and the gas combined (F_b > F_g), causing it to float.
By hanging the balloon filled with air with a string to the helium balloon, you increase its total downward gravitational vector until it matches the upward atmosphere lift exactly (F_b = F_g). With a net force of zero, it achieves static equilibrium and hovers.

### Concepts ###
* Archimedes' Principle: The physical law dictating that any object immersed in a fluid is buoyed upward by a force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces.
* Density: The measure of mass per unit volume. Objects or gases less dense than their surrounding medium will float; those that are denser will sink.
* Neutral Buoyancy: The precise condition where an object's net average density is exactly equal to the density of the surrounding fluid, causing it to hover seamlessly.
* Static Equilibrium: A state of rest achieved when the vector sum of all opposing forces acting on a system equals exactly zero (F = 0).
