### Description ###
A double cone, when placed between two angled rods, will climb up the rods due to the its shape.

### Equipment ###
* double cone
* rods attachment
* riser
* level

### Instruction ###
Place the rods attachment on the riser (catalogue) such that the split end is raised higher. Show that this end is higher using the level. Place the double cone on the lowered end and watch it roll (climb) upwards towards the split end.

### Physics ###
Although the tracks physically rise higher into the air, the rods also diverge. As the double cone rolls forward, the widening tracks make contact with the cone closer and closer to its narrow tips rather than its wide centre. Because the tracks open up, the wide centre section of the double cone drops down between the rods. The drop caused the centre of mass of the cone actually to move downward toward throughout its entire journey. Nature always seeks the lowest energy state. The cone moves forward because dropping its centre of mass converts gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy, perfectly satisfying the law of motion.

### Concepts ###
* Center of Mass: The singular point in space where the entire distribution of an object's mass is located.
* Gravitational Potential Energy: The energy stored in an object due to its height. The double cone rolls forward because the height of its centre of mass is decreasing, minimizing its potential energy.
