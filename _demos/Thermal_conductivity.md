### Description ###
A star-shaped conductor with spokes made of different metals—such as copper, aluminum, brass, steel, and stainless steel—is heated at its central hub, melting wax or triggering temperature indicators at the tips at different times to show varying rates of heat conduction.

### Equipment ###
* Thermal conductivity star (conductor with a central brass hub and radiating spokes of copper, aluminum, brass, steel, and stainless steel)
* Paraffin wax pellets, birthday candle shavings, or liquid crystal temperature strips
* Heat source (Bunsen burner, alcohol lamp, or propane torch)
* Heat-resistant mat

### Instruction ###
* Initial setup: Place an equal-sized pellet of paraffin wax into the small dimple at the outer tip of each metal spoke (or attach thermochromic strips along each rod).
* Heating the hub: Hold the conductor horizontally by its insulated handle and position the central metal hub directly over the burner flame so all spokes receive heat equally from the center.
* Observing conduction rates: Watch the tips of the spokes carefully. The wax on the copper spoke will melt first, followed by aluminum, brass, carbon steel, and finally stainless steel—demonstrating how quickly heat travels through each metal.
* Cooling down: Turn off the heat source and place the apparatus on a heat-resistant mat to cool completely before handling or wiping off melted wax.

### Physics ###
Thermal conduction is the transfer of internal thermal energy through direct collisions between neighbouring atoms and the movement of free electrons within a material. Metals are good thermal conductors because they contain a high density of delocalized free electrons that rapidly carry kinetic energy from the hot central hub toward the cooler outer tips. However, each metal has a distinct crystal lattice structure and electron mobility, giving it a specific thermal conductivity constant ($k$). Copper ($k \approx 400\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) conducts heat more than twenty times faster than stainless steel ($k \approx 15\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$), meaning thermal energy reaches the end of the copper spoke and melts its wax long before the stainless steel tip warms up.

### Concepts ###
* Thermal conduction: the transfer of heat energy through a solid material without any bulk movement of the material itself.
* Thermal conductivity ($k$): a material-specific property measuring how easily heat flows through a substance (governed by Fourier's Law, $\frac{Q}{t} = kA\frac{\Delta T}{L}$).
* Free-electron heat transport: in metals, mobile valence electrons collide and transport thermal energy much faster than lattice vibrations (phonons) alone.
