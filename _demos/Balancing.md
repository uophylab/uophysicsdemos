### Description ###
The centre of mass of irregular objects allows these objects to balance themselves in precarious positions.


### Equipment ###
* utensils
* glass
* eagle
* cone

### Instruction ###
Balance the utensils arrangement on the edge of the glass. Balance the eagle's beak on the tip of the inverted cone. (see images and movies)

### Physics ###
For an object to remain balanced on a narrow pivot, its total centre of mass must align perfectly on a vertical line straight through the point of support. In both setups, the physical shapes are highly irregular, creating a visual illusion of instability. However, because the heavy handles of the utensils curve far past the edge of the glass, and the eagle's weighted wingtips sweep far forward past its beak, the position of the centre of mass is pushed outside the physical structure, hanging in mid-air directly beneath the pivot point.  Because the centre of mass sits below the actual point of support, any slight tilt or disturbance forces the centre of mass to rise. Gravity immediately creates a restoring torque that pulls the centre of mass back down to its lowest possible potential energy state, automatically correcting the tilt and maintaining balance.

### Concepts ###
*  Centre of Mass: The singular point in space where the entire distribution of an object's mass averages out.
*  Restoring Torque: The rotational force generated by gravity when an object tilts, acting as a pendulum effect that swings the centre of mass straight back under the fulcrum.
