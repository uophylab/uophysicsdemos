### Description ###
A lightweight ping pong ball coated in conductive paint or foil hangs from an insulating thread and reacts to a nearby charged rod—first swinging toward it via electrostatic induction, then violently repelling away after making contact and acquiring the same charge.

### Equipment ###
* Ping pong ball coated with conductive graphite/nickel paint or wrapped smoothly in aluminum foil
* Non-conducting thread (silk or nylon)
* Insulated support stand
* Acrylic, PVC, or glass charging rod
* Wool cloth, fur, or silk pad (for triboelectric charging)

### Instruction ###
* Initial setup: Suspend the conductive ping pong ball from the insulated support stand using the non-conducting thread so it hangs freely at rest away from other objects.
* Charging the rod: Rub the PVC or acrylic rod briskly with the wool cloth or fur to build up a static electric charge.
* Electrostatic attraction (induction): Slowly bring the charged rod near the hanging ping pong ball without touching it. Observe the neutral ball swing toward the rod.
* Charge transfer and repulsion: Allow the ball to briefly touch the charged rod. The instant contact is made, watch the ball snap sharply away from the rod and stay repelled as the rod is moved closer.
* Grounding (resetting): Touch the ping pong ball with your finger to neutralize its excess charge and return it to its vertical resting position.

### Physics ###
When a negatively charged rod approaches the neutral conductive ping pong ball, free electrons on the conductive coating are repelled to the far side of the ball, leaving a net positive charge on the side closest to the rod (electrostatic induction). Because the positive side is closer to the rod than the negative side, the attractive Coulomb force is stronger than the repulsive force, pulling the ball toward the rod. The moment the ball touches the rod, excess electrons jump onto the conductive surface, giving the ball the same net negative charge as the rod. Since like charges repel, the ball immediately springs away and remains repelled until grounded.

### Concepts ###
* Electrostatic induction: the redistribution of electrical charges within a neutral conductor caused by the nearby presence of a charged object, resulting in temporary attraction.
* Charging by conduction (contact): the direct transfer of electrons between two objects that touch, leaving both with the same sign of net electric charge.
* Coulomb's Law: opposite charges attract and like charges repel with a force inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them ($F = k\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$).
